KEY ANALYTICAL FINDINGS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. 71.6% of events are normal, but Critical (14.6%) and Warning (13.7%) alerts are almost evenly split - severe incidents are a steady share of traffic, not rare outliers.
2. The highest-impact event (Train / East of Hudson) had a 33.3-minute delay but cost 119.8 passenger-hours - about 31.2x a comparable-delay event, due to onboard load.
3. Critical-tier delays cluster near the threshold rather than becoming runaway outliers.
4. Low speed is a strong leading indicator of high passenger impact.
5. Alert burden is spread across corridors rather than concentrated in one location.

CONCLUSION
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The real-time dashboard combines live KPI cards, a rolling delay trajectory, corridor burden charts, an automated threshold incident feed and severity diagnostics. Across 990 monitored vehicles it flagged 281 active alerts against a 9.87-minute average delay and 13,869 cumulative passenger-hours lost, showing that low speed and high passenger load - not delay duration alone - drive the largest-impact incidents. The Streamlit app in practicals/app_dashboard.py serves the same view interactively.
